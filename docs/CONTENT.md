# Content and source notes

## Foundation

The owner's editorial baseline is the [28 fundamental beliefs published by the General Conference](https://gc.adventist.org/beliefs/), consulted 2026-09-12. The belief numbers in each study are editorial cross-references, not visible lessons or statements of exhaustive coverage.

The guide contains original explanatory prose and reading questions. It does not reproduce the belief statements or quote Ellen White. The Bible is the reader-facing source; references are provided next to the relevant prompts. An optional online Bible helper constructs KJV or BSB passage links only when requested.

Primary Scripture reference checks included the KJV text at Bible Hub, including [John 11](https://biblehub.com/kjv/john/11.htm), [Ecclesiastes 9](https://biblehub.com/kjv/ecclesiastes/9.htm), [Ephesians 2](https://biblehub.com/kjv/ephesians/2.htm), [Exodus 34](https://biblehub.com/kjv/exodus/34.htm), [Acts 17](https://biblehub.com/kjv/acts/17.htm), [Mark 2](https://biblehub.com/kjv/mark/2.htm), and [1 Thessalonians 4](https://biblehub.com/kjv/1_thessalonians/4.htm). Each exploration lists its complete assigned passages in `src/content.js`.

This is the initial authored collection. Owner review and broader passage-by-passage editorial review remain before production. The code does not claim an independent theological certification or comprehensive treatment of difficult countertexts.

## Authoring conventions

1. Start with the visitor's question. Use a specific, searchable title and common-language search phrases.
2. Give references to meaningful passage units. Read across chapter boundaries when the argument continues.
3. Invite observation before presenting the optional synthesis. Distinguish textual observations from the guide's interpretation.
4. Positively explain the biblical case, including why it offers insight into God's character and hope. Do not manufacture assurance about the cause of an individual's experience.
5. Offer related questions as choices. No next-lesson lock, scores, invisible agreement assessment, or required declaration of belief.
6. Keep the two writing prompts optional. The visitor can disagree, remain uncertain, or write about something else.
7. Preserve existing IDs when content is expanded. Journal entries hold study IDs and a copy of the examined references, so prior research remains recognizable.
8. Add search regression cases for important new questions and verify all related-study IDs resolve.
